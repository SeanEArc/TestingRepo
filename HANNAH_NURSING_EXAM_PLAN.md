# Hannah's nursing exam study plan

This is a four-week starting plan for an NCLEX-style nursing licensure exam. If Hannah is taking a course final or another exam, keep the daily method and replace the topic list with her instructor's exam blueprint. Adjust the pace to the actual test date and her available hours.

## Set up (day 1)

1. Confirm the exam name (NCLEX-RN, NCLEX-PN, or a school exam), date, and content outline. For NCLEX, use the [current RN or PN test plan](https://www.nclex.com/test-plans.page) to identify tested areas and clinical judgment expectations.
2. Take a timed, mixed baseline quiz from a reputable question bank. Record performance by topic and question type. Use the results to choose the two weakest areas; do not rely on an overall score alone.
3. Put six study blocks and one lighter review/rest day on the calendar each week. Start with 2–3 focused hours per study day, split into short blocks with breaks. Adjust for work, school, and fatigue.

## Repeat this study routine on each study day

- **Recall (20 minutes):** Without notes, write what you remember about yesterday's weak topic, then check and correct it.
- **Focused review (45–60 minutes):** Study one content area from the exam outline. Make a short list of key assessments, safety actions, patient teaching, and common medication risks.
- **Practice (45–60 minutes):** Answer 25–40 mixed and topic-specific questions, including case studies. Work under timed conditions on at least three days each week.
- **Rationale review (30–45 minutes):** Review every missed or guessed question. For each, write the cue you missed, why the correct response is safest, and one rule you can apply next time. Revisit that log after two days and one week.

## Four-week schedule

| Week | Focus | End-of-week check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Baseline; safety and infection prevention; fundamentals, assessment, and prioritization. Practice identifying urgent versus expected findings. | Repeat a mixed quiz and identify the two weakest categories. |
| 2 | Adult health: respiratory, cardiac, neurologic, endocrine, renal, and fluid/electrolyte problems. Add medication administration and adverse-effect questions. | Explain the rationale for missed questions without looking at notes. |
| 3 | Maternal/newborn, pediatrics, mental health, health promotion, and patient teaching. Keep reviewing the weakest adult-health topics. | Complete a longer timed mixed set and review the full error log. |
| 4 | Mixed questions and clinical-judgment case studies. Target persistent gaps; practice pacing and exam-day routine. Reduce study volume in the final 24 hours and sleep normally. | Complete one full-length or realistic timed practice exam early in the week, then focus on its patterns of errors. |

## Clinical judgment practice

For each case study, say aloud: What cues matter? What problem is most likely? What needs attention first? What action is safe? What outcome would show improvement? The [official NCLEX sample pack and exam preview](https://www.nclex.com/prepare.page) show the current item styles.

## Progress rule

At the end of each week, compare results by content area and question type. Spend the next week's first two focused review blocks on the lowest areas. If performance falls, shorten content review and spend more time analyzing rationales; if timing is the problem, add timed mixed sets. Practice scores are a guide to gaps, not a guarantee of passing.

## Final 48 hours and exam day

Check the appointment, route, and identification requirements in the [candidate bulletin](https://www.ncsbn.org/publications/2026-nclex-candidate-bulletin). Do a short review of the error log, prepare what is permitted, and avoid a last-minute cram session. For NCLEX, the [exam-day guidance](https://www.nclex.com/exam-day.page) says to arrive at least 30 minutes early and notes that the exam can take up to five hours including breaks.
